## BEFORE YOU READ THIS

This document exists so that anyone who touches this brand — an editor, a photographer, a VA, a future team member — can pick it up and immediately understand not just what Moss & Mail *is*, but how it *sounds*, how it *feels*, and what it is absolutely not allowed to become.

The voice of this brand is the owner's voice. It is warm without being saccharine. It is honest without being heavy. It is beautiful without being performative. Every word published under this brand should feel like it came from a real woman sitting across from you at a kitchen table, with something worth saying.

When in doubt, ask: *Does this sound like a letter from a friend who actually sees me?* If yes, publish it. If no, rewrite it.

## SECTION 1 — BRAND VOICE STATEMENT

Kayli Angeleaux speaks the way a trusted friend writes a letter: unhurried, honest, and paying close attention to the things most people rush past. Her voice is warm but never cloying, poetic but never pretentious — she finds the beautiful in the ordinary and offers it to you like something she picked up on a walk and thought you'd want to see. On camera she speaks as if she's talking directly to one person she trusts completely. She loves to read, and when she shares a poem or a quote, it feels like a gift rather than a performance. She does not perform. She does not polish. She shows up as herself, and that is the whole point.

---

## SECTION 2 — BRAND MISSION

Moss & Mail exists to slow things down — to put something beautiful, handwritten, and full of care into the hands of women who are yearning for something slower, something tangible, something in their mailbox — not on a screen. One letter at a time. One mailbox at a time. One heart at a time.

---

## SECTION 3 — BRAND PROMISE

Every month, when that envelope arrives, you will feel like someone thought of you specifically. Not you as a demographic. Not you as a subscriber number. *You* — the one who is tired and trying, who loves beautiful things and hasn't had much time for them lately, who sometimes just needs to know that somewhere, someone is living slowly and deliberately and wanted to bring you along.

Inside the envelope you will always find:

- A **handwritten letter** from Kayli — composed in the wilds of her Utah mountains, signed by hand, on beautiful stationery, sealed with a wax seal. Vulnerable and honest — the beauty she is finding, what she is struggling with, what she is mulling over. A letter from a friend.
- A **curated Spotify playlist** QR code — to enjoy while opening your envelope
- An "**Ask Kayli**" Q&A — questions submitted by subscribers on social media, answered by Kayli
- A **nature-inspired journaling prompt/conversation starter**
- A **simple, nourishing recipe**
- A **photo from the mountains** or a watercolor from Kayli's children — one of a kind

Plus seasonal curated extras — chosen for the month, the mood, the season. Some things come back. Some things only come once. Everything fits in the envelope. Under one ounce. Packed with intention and beauty. Prepared by a loving human for you.

But what you are really receiving is what Kayli has always felt since she was a child running to the mailbox — that rush of magic when something arrives with your name on it. Someone sat down, picked up a pen, and thought of you. Those words were written *just* for you. You are connected to someone from far away, sharing pieces of life you can hold onto forever. You are thought of. You are cared about. That is the gift in the mailbox.

## SECTION 4 — THE FIVE CONTENT PILLARS

*Every post, every video, every caption lives under one of these five themes.*

---

### PILLAR 1 — THE SLOW LIFE IN REAL TIME

This is documentation, not curation. Real mornings, real kitchens, real chaos followed by real stillness. Not the highlight reel of a slow life — the actual practice of choosing it, imperfectly, every day.

**Content examples:** Making breakfast at a messy counter. Resetting the homeschool table after a hard morning. Walking barefoot in wet grass. Letting a child lead the pace on a nature walk. The iced rose matcha latte made for the third time because it still brings joy.

**Why it matters:** The audience wants a slower life but feels like they can't have it because their circumstances aren't perfect. This pillar proves the slower life isn't a destination — it's a series of small, choosable moments available right now.

---

### PILLAR 2 — THE NATURAL WORLD AS TEACHER

Nature is not a backdrop here — it is a curriculum. This pillar is about watching, noticing, and learning from what grows and moves and builds around us. Wonder first, knowledge second.

**Content examples:** Explaining the difference between a beaver dam and a beaver den. Pointing out moss and lichen. Pressing flowers. Understanding why heirloom seeds matter. Watching spring happen in real time.

**Why it matters:** Speaks to homeschool moms who want enrichment, women in apartments who feel disconnected from growing things, and grandmothers who want to pass something real to their grandchildren.

---

### PILLAR 3 — HANDS THAT MAKE THINGS

The act of making — cooking, writing, growing, pressing, creating — is an act of resistance against a life that has become too fast, too digital, and too disposable.

**Content examples:** Recipe card content. Cooking videos. Writing a letter by hand. Wax seals. A child's artwork. Planting seeds. Making something from scratch that could have been bought ready-made.

**Why it matters:** Most directly connected to the subscription. Builds desire for the physical product by building love for the act of making. Speaks to the audience's deep longing: *I want my life to feel handmade.*

---

### PILLAR 4 — YOU ARE NOT ALONE

This is the heart pillar. It speaks directly to the isolation, overwhelm, and quiet grief that lives in the audience. It does not fix anything. It simply witnesses. It says: *I see what you're carrying. I'm carrying things too. We're in this together.*

**Content examples:** Honest captions about hard days. The Ohio house story. Posts written directly to one woman in one specific moment. Acknowledgment of the season of life the audience is in — without pity.

**Why it matters:** This pillar is what turns followers into subscribers and subscribers into a community that stays. Women who feel seen do not leave.

---

### PILLAR 5 — BEAUTY AS A DAILY PRACTICE

Beauty is not luxury. It is not something you earn when circumstances improve. It is something you can choose right now, in whatever space you have, with whatever you have in it.

**Content examples:** Aesthetic photography that doesn't feel staged. Color stories. Seasonal moments. The shapes and textures she loves. Classical music as a mood. A kintsugi bowl that is more beautiful for being broken and repaired.

**Why it matters:** Speaks to the woman who is starving for beauty but has been told it is frivolous. Gently, persistently reframes beauty as nourishment, not decoration.

## SECTION 5 — PLATFORM VOICE GUIDE

*The same woman. Different rooms.*

---

### INSTAGRAM — A letter left on the kitchen table

The most literary of the platforms. Captions can be long — this audience will read. Write like you mean every word. Use line breaks generously. Leave white space.

- **Works:** Soft beautiful photography, close-up textures, seasonal palettes, carousels that tell a small story, captions that begin mid-thought
- **Doesn't work:** Trending audio, graphics heavy with text, urgency language, hashtag stacking at the end of a beautiful caption
- **CTA style:** Gentle. "If this reached you today, I'd love to hear why." Never: "LINK IN BIO!!!"

---

### TIKTOK — A chance conversation that turns unexpectedly real

More conversational, slightly more immediate. Think out loud. Do NOT open with a manufactured hook — open with the truth of what she's doing or thinking. The right people will stay.

- **Works:** Unscripted present-tense content, talking while doing something, walking and talking in nature, real reactions to real moments (the beaver dam video is perfect native TikTok)
- **Doesn't work:** Trending dances, "POV:" formats forced unnaturally, rapid cuts and text-over-video in the influencer format
- **CTA style:** Offhand, natural. "I'm putting together a monthly letter club if that sounds like your kind of thing."

---

### FACEBOOK — A community table where multiple generations sit

Warmer and slightly more direct. The grandmother audience and older millennial audience lives here most comfortably. More plainspoken while keeping the warmth.

- **Works:** Longer-form personal stories, the Ohio house narrative, YouTube shares, posts that invite real conversation, questions about what people are growing or cooking
- **Doesn't work:** Meme formats, overly casual language, ignoring comments
- **CTA style:** Warm and direct. "I'd love for you to be on my list." "If you know someone who needs a letter, share this with them."

---

### YOUTUBE — An invitation into her home

The most expansive platform. The most space to be fully herself. The previous renovation channel is the foundation — an audience that already watched her navigate difficulty and beauty and loss.

- **Works:** Videos that document rather than perform, process content, personal essays in video form, the Ohio house as origin story, season-based content
- **Doesn't work:** Hard fast cuts, clickbait thumbnails, overly produced intros, forcing herself into a format that doesn't fit her
- **CTA style:** Woven into the content naturally. "I'm building something I want to tell you about" — not tacked on at the end as an obligation

## SECTION 6 — DO / DO NOT

### THE BRAND ALWAYS:
1. Speaks to one woman, not an audience — every post is addressed to a single person
2. Shows the real thing, not the ideal version
3. Moves at its own pace — does not accelerate because an algorithm suggests it
4. Gives before it asks — every post offers something before mentioning the subscription
5. Names the hard thing without making it the whole story
6. Credits the child's contributions as genuine collaborations, not props
7. Uses the natural world as a consistent thread across all platforms
8. Responds to comments and messages as if they are letters received
9. Maintains visual consistency in color, light, and aesthetic
10. Tells the whole story over time — documents the ongoing practice

### THE BRAND NEVER:
1. Uses urgency language — "limited time," "don't miss out," "before it's too late"
2. Chases trends for algorithmic performance
3. Performs emotions it doesn't feel
4. Talks down to the audience about their choices or circumstances
5. Over-explains or apologizes for sharing
6. Uses the child as content without regard for the child's dignity
7. Posts something just to post something — silence is not failure, empty content is
8. Pretends the difficult things didn't happen
9. Compares itself to other subscription boxes or brands
10. Lets the product become the only story — the brand is the journey, not just the destination

## SECTION 7 — THE OHIO HOUSE STORY

### Why this is not a liability — it is the most powerful story she has

The Ohio house story is about a woman who saw beauty in something broken, who poured herself into something real, who encountered obstacles that would have stopped most people, who kept going anyway, and who ultimately had to let go of something she loved — and survived it.

The woman who subscribes to Moss & Mail is often carrying her own version of this story. When she hears the Ohio house story, she does not hear failure. She hears herself.

---

### HOW TO INTRODUCE IT — THREE PHASES

**Phase 1 — The Reference (First 30 days)**
Do not tell the full story yet. Let it appear as a natural detail in other content.
*"I used to think my forever home was a 150-year-old house in Ohio... anyway, that's a longer story."*
This creates curiosity without making loss the first thing she says about herself. The audience leans in.

**Phase 2 — The First Full Telling (YouTube, first major personal video)**
The Ohio house gets its own video. Not a crisis video. A quiet, honest documentary-style telling.

Suggested structure:
- Open with what she loved about it — the vision, the bones, the dream. Make the audience fall in love before they know what happens.
- Introduce the complications — the contractor, the difficulty — with honesty but without bitterness
- The moment she knew she had to let it go. Hold this. Don't rush it.
- What she understood on the other side — not a tidy lesson, something real and hard-won: *I learned that I can love something completely and still release it. I think Moss & Mail was always where I was going.*
- Close by connecting directly to the mission of the brand

**Phase 3 — The Living Thread (Ongoing)**
Return to it naturally — not as a content category, but as a quiet presence — when talking about why handmade things matter, the kintsugi spirit, letting something go, the audience going through hard things.

**What to never do:** Do not dramatize it for engagement. Do not post "The TRUTH about why I had to sell my dream house" with a shocked-face thumbnail. Tell it once, well. Let it breathe.

## SECTION 8 — THREE AUDIENCE PERSONAS

### CAROLINE, 34
Two-bedroom apartment, two kids ages 4 and 7, works part-time from home. Grew up with a garden, has spent a decade telling herself she'll "get back to that" when circumstances are better. Follows slow-living accounts and feels quietly embarrassed her life doesn't look like them.

**What she's struggling with:** The gap between the life she imagined and the life she has.

**What Moss & Mail gives her:** Permission. Something arrives that says the slow life is not somewhere else. She reads the letter during the 45 minutes after drop-off, before she opens her laptop. It is the best part of her month.

---

### DIANE, 61
Raising her 9-year-old granddaughter full-time. Stepped in without hesitation but feels isolated — doesn't know many grandmothers in her situation. Found the brand while looking for activities that didn't involve screens.

**What she's struggling with:** Isolation, uncertainty about raising a child in 2026, a quiet longing for beauty and meaning.

**What Moss & Mail gives her:** Company and a curriculum. The letter makes her feel seen. The seeds become a project she and her granddaughter do together. The child's artwork on the card makes her granddaughter feel connected to another child's creativity.

---

### MAYA, 29
Single mom to a 3-year-old. Works full-time as a dental hygienist, loosely homeschools evenings and weekends. Exhausted, competent, does not ask for help easily. Found the brand on TikTok, watched three videos, felt something unexpected: relief.

**What she's struggling with:** The relentlessness of doing everything alone. Always in execution mode. Gives constantly and receives almost nothing meant for her.

**What Moss & Mail gives her:** Something that is hers. The envelope is addressed to her. She presses the flowers from the first envelope into a book and keeps it on her nightstand. She tells her best friend. Her best friend signs up.

| Font | Use For |
|------|---------|

| **True Typewriter** | Everything else — captions, quotes, recipe cards, overlays, headings, letter-style text |

**The rule is simple:**
- See "Moss & Mail" as a name → Beth Ellen
- Most everything else → True Typewriter
- Needs to be plain and easy to read → Droid Serif

### Photography & Video Style
Natural light only. Warm tones over cool. Real textures — wood grain, worn fabric, soil, pressed petals, aged paper. Imperfect framing is acceptable; sterile perfection is not. The aesthetic is *found*, not constructed. Think Beatrix Potter's world made real — the garden, the worn table, the letter half-written.

### Color Grading Note
Kayli wants some content to have the warm, film-like, desaturated-but-warm quality of @MiaEnglishRoseCottage, who shoots in V-Log. To achieve this without V-Log capability, use:
1. **CapCut** — "Film" or "Retro" preset filters, then lower saturation slightly and add warmth
2. **Lightroom Mobile** (free) — preset packs like "Film Grain Warm" or "Moody Earth Tones"
3. **VSCO** — filters A4, A6, or HB1 approximate the warm, faded film look
Raw, ungraded content is also completely on-brand — not every video needs color treatment.

## A FINAL NOTE

This brand was built from a real woman's real life. The slowness is not a brand strategy — it is a value she holds. The warmth is not a tone choice — it is who she is. The Ohio house is not a content opportunity — it is a piece of her history she is trusting the world with.

Handle all of it accordingly.

When something feels off — when a post is almost right but something is nagging — come back here and ask:
*Does this sound like a letter from a friend who sees you?*

If yes, send it. If no, start again.
